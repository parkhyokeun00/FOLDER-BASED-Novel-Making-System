# Longform Expansion Protocol

Use this when a story seed proves it has an engine, but the draft still behaves like a short story.

Its job is to expand a compact engine into a readable volume rhythm without bloating it.

## Expansion signs

Run this when two or more are true:

- the concept is strong, but the draft closes too quickly
- chapters function well but the volume still feels undersized
- the story has a beginning and ending image but too little middle pressure
- the draft keeps collapsing into summary instead of sustained movement

## Expansion path

1. define the volume question again at long-form scale
2. break the spine into 8 to 12 chapter-sized movements
3. assign one mid-volume complication or false lead
4. assign one late-volume bridge or collapse chapter
5. confirm the ending remains the same kind of ending, only better earned

## Mandatory scale checks

### Launch check

Before chapter 1:

- define the expected chapter band or movement count
- define what would prove this is genuinely long-form rather than compressed shortform

### Chapter-003 check

At the close of chapter 3, ask:

- does the book still feel like it has runway
- are middle pressures actually alive
- is the draft still expanding, not only summarizing
- is the planned ending being earned rather than rushed toward

If the answer is weak, pause drafting and expand before continuing.

## Rule

Do not add chapters only to increase count.
Add chapters only when they create:

- deeper scene life
- stronger cause-and-effect pressure
- more human cost
- cleaner commercial rhythm
