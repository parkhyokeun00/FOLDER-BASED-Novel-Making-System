# Contaminated Evidence Pattern

Use this when the story depends on a diary, testimony, transcript, footage, note, confession, or other record.

The record should not behave like an answer key.

## Three-stage pattern

### 1. Trust

Present the evidence as useful, legible, and apparently forward-moving.

The reader should think:

- this helps
- this explains something
- this is worth following

### 2. Fracture

Introduce controlled instability through:

- date mismatch
- diction mismatch
- missing pages or gaps
- physical handling traces
- external records that do not align
- details that feel too neat or too corrected

### 3. Inversion

Reveal that the evidence was coached, edited, arranged, rewritten, or selectively preserved.

The strongest version is not pure forgery.
It is truth mixed with guided language.

## Rules

- never let the record replace the protagonist's own verification work
- do not make the evidence fully false unless unfairness is the point
- do not make the evidence fully clean if the story wants suspense
- keep at least one core truth inside the contaminated record
- plant enough friction that a reread feels earned, not tricked

## Good contamination signals

- the object was discovered too neatly
- the wording sounds more polished than the writer should be
- page order or time order is wrong
- one repeated phrase seems borrowed from another speaker
- the material object shows pressure, erasure, replacement, or staging

## Check

Ask:

1. Why does the reader trust this evidence at first?
2. What exact detail starts the fracture?
3. What survives as true after the inversion?
4. What does the protagonist learn by testing the world against the record?
