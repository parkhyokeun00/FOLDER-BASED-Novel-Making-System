# Narrator Motive Early Check

Use this when the POV begins as an observer, witness, reporter, detective, manager, neighbor, clerk, or other seemingly detached figure.

The point is to answer early:
Why does this case catch on this person?

## Minimum early proof

By chapter 1 or the first scene cluster, show at least one of these:

- a private bruise, bias, or unfinished history
- a vow or refusal pattern
- a cost they will pay if they ignore this
- a line or habit showing they react harder than a neutral observer would

## Rule

One sentence or one behavior is enough.
Do not dump biography.

## Avoid

- saving the narrator's reason for caring until the middle
- explaining the whole backstory before the plot starts
- leaving the POV as a camera when the story needs a witness with skin in the game

## Check questions

1. What makes this event personally intolerable to the narrator?
2. Why can they not simply walk away?
3. What small early detail proves that?
4. Does this detail deepen the story without hijacking it?

## Result

If no good answer appears, either:

- strengthen the narrator's hook
- change the POV
- or lower the amount of investigation the narrator is expected to carry
